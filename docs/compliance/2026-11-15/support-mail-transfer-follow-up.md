# Consumer Gmail support: service-specific follow-up

Reviewed November 15, 2026. This is a follow-up to the [earlier assessment](support-and-data-safety-final.md), not a change to the immutable rc8 app or source archive. The maintainer requested completion of support-mail transfer conditions and encrypted signing-key backup, and explicitly deferred the authenticated Claude test. A deferred test remains unverified.

## Confirmed service facts

The selected support mailbox is ordinary free Gmail, with no automatic forwarding. Google's [Korean consumer terms](https://policies.google.com/terms?hl=ko&gl=kr) identify Google LLC. Its US corporate address does not establish US-only processing. The [Korean privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy?hl=ko&gl=kr), effective October 16, 2026, covers received email and international processing, and publishes `googlekrsupport@google.com` as a privacy contact.

Google's [Privacy Help Center](https://support.google.com/policies/answer/9581826?hl=en) explicitly distinguishes consumer Gmail/Drive from enterprise offerings: it does not provide a Data Processing Agreement for those consumer services and does not describe itself as their data processor. Consequently, a Workspace processing agreement cannot be attributed to this free mailbox. Google's terminology alone does not decide the operator's role or legality under Korean law.

The [general data-center directory](https://datacenters.google/locations/) describes Google's facilities, including construction projects. It is not a service-specific list of every country handling this mailbox's messages, backups or remote support. Neither a US-only assertion nor a copied list of every Google facility resolves the current evidence gap.

## Applicable review boundary

[PIPA Article 28-8](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1029334743) provides several transfer routes, including separate consent and necessary contract-related outsourcing/storage with notice. A paid customer contract is not inherently required. If a route applies, its factual and disclosure conditions still need to be met; the operator's acceptance of Google's own terms is not the sender's separate transfer consent.

The [PIPC's current April 2026 privacy-policy guidance](https://pipc.go.kr/np/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?bbsId=BS217&mCode=D010030000&nttId=12018) calls for concrete applicable country/recipient/purpose/retention particulars, including relevant onward processing. This means establishing the service's documented processing scope, not tracking every Internet transit hop or auditing Google's unrelated businesses.

The [PIPC distinction between direct collection and a transfer](https://pipc.go.kr/np/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?bbsId=BS292&mCode=I040010000&nttId=10972) matters: Google's own direct collection when a person signs up is different from a Korean operator's use of external storage for its support work. No official ruling about this exact free-Gmail support arrangement was found. Treating continued operator storage as needing assessment is an inference from the general framework, not a regulator's finding that this mailbox is unlawful.

## Finite outcome and maintainer decision

The remaining item is still **not established by the reviewed public documents**: the consumer-Gmail country scope and a substantiated Article 28-8 route for this support workflow. Adding a checkbox with an invented country would not close it. The existing minimum-data, no-forwarding, 90-day post-resolution retention and deletion procedures remain applicable.

A narrowly scoped question was prepared locally for Google's published Korean privacy contact, but the maintainer explicitly chose not to send it and requested continued preparation. No inquiry was sent, no response or approval was obtained, and no work depends on waiting for such a response. The local draft is historical preparation, not a pending authorization request.

A paid or domestic-brand mailbox is not automatically a verified replacement. A short review of Hiworks confirmed a one-person product, but did not establish all service-specific message-storage, backup and support-access terms from the reviewed public documentation. No account, subscription, domain purchase or migration was made. A replacement needs its applicable service documents assessed before the public support address and policy are changed.

Source, app and store preparation continue under that instruction. This bounded support-operation uncertainty is not a finding that the app cannot be submitted or a requirement for advance vendor approval. It does not establish complete support-processing compliance or close the separate Play account/declaration steps. The existing minimal-data and retention procedures continue; the support address and public policy have not been changed to assert unsupported processing facts.
